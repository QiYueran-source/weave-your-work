# Keep Project Design in Maps

A map is the durable record of a project target and the decisions that shape it. Keep design work attached to that target so later tasks and reviews have a stable source.

## What to do

1. Before a project-design discussion or grill, find the map that owns the design. Create one when none exists, then link the discussion's testimony to it.
2. As the design settles, write each decision, changed target, or exclusion into the owning map. Do this during the discussion, not as a summary at the end.
3. Before splitting or reviewing a map, follow the map procedure and the skills it names.
4. Edit an open map with no children directly. For a map that has children, or a closed map, use the update skill. Update checks what relies on the map and whether any recorded verdict still holds.
5. If design work advanced before a map existed, record what actually happened and the evidence now available. Do not describe a later ticket as a plan that existed beforehand, or record a review that did not happen.

Read-only investigation and pure consultation do not need a map unless their findings become a project decision or a target the team will pursue.

## Why

A design detached from its map can be lost when the conversation ends. The map lets the team trace why its tasks exist and what their results must satisfy.
