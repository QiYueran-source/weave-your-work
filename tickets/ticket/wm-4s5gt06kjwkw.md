## What to build

Someone reading the opening of `to-spec` finds three steps for turning a
discussion into a spec, and every one of them is about office work. The first
reads the project's own records and documents instead of a codebase. The second
settles what is being handed over and how a reader will know it was handed over,
and confirms both with the person before anything is written. The third writes
the spec in the project's vocabulary and publishes it.

The words a code repository put there are gone. A reader finds no instruction to
explore a repo, no request to name a seam, and no reference to a test.

The skill keeps Matt's shape and its two refusals: it does not interview the
person who asked for it, and the spec it writes carries no place names.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Step 1 tells the reader to read the project's current state, naming the
      open specs, the tickets, the glossary, the decision log, and any document
      the change touches, and says the vocabulary comes from that read.
- [ ] Step 1 keeps Matt's instruction not to re-explore what was already read in
      this session.
- [ ] Step 2 asks for the delivery and for the judgement on it, in office words:
      the delivery is judged at the finished thing rather than at a step toward
      it, and the judgement is one line where possible.
- [ ] Step 2 keeps the instruction to confirm both with the person.
- [ ] Step 2 does not mention seams, and carries no instruction to prefer an
      existing one.
- [ ] Step 3 writes the spec, applies the template, and publishes it to the
      tracker.
- [ ] The three steps contain no occurrence of "codebase", "seam", or "test".
- [ ] The skill still says it does not interview the person, and still forbids
      place names in the spec body.
