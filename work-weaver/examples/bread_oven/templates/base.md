# The baker's handbook

Written by a person, read by the chain, never changed by it. It says which
knobs move which features and what shape each relation has. It says no numbers
for where a knob should sit: that is found by baking and reading the score.

## Knobs and the features they dominate

- `oven_temperature` dominates crispness. Crispness rises as the oven heats,
  peaks, then falls as the loaf dries and scorches.
- `bake_time` dominates chewiness. Chewiness rises with time to a firm peak, and
  a long bake turns the crumb dry and hard.
- `proofing_time` dominates softness. Softness rises as the dough proofs, peaks,
  then falls as over-proofed dough collapses and bakes dense.
- `water_amount` dominates moistness. Moistness rises with water, peaks where the
  crumb is open but not wet, then falls into a gummy loaf.
- `sugar_amount` dominates sweetness. Sweetness rises with sugar, peaks where the
  crust browns evenly, then falls as the sugar burns.

Every knob also moves the other four features a little, always along the same
peaked shape. Turning one knob to its own peak does not put the others at theirs.

## Limits

- A bake far shorter, or an oven far lower, than bread needs leaves the loaf
  raw; far above, it scorches. Both ends of a knob's range are failures, not
  choices.
- Only these five knobs exist. A bake sets their values; it cannot add a knob,
  change a range, or reshape the schedule.
- Each bake is judged by a taste that differs a little from the last one. A
  single bake's score is noisy; several rounds tell you more than one.
